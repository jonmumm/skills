# The idea funnel (going wide across games)

1. **Generate 20+ ideas** in IDEAS.md. Each is one paragraph: the fantasy, what the kid does, what
   the grown-up does, what the TV shows, why they'd talk, and what's different from the catalog.
   Sources: board games the family likes, kid obsessions from playtests, X posts Jon likes, the
   gaps in the catalog.
2. **Score each idea** 1–5 on the studio rubric rows that apply on paper:
   - kid alone after one demo
   - talking together
   - little-kid delight
   - TV spectacle potential
   - replay pull
   - distinctness
   - build risk (inverted)

   Two independent scorer subagents; take the mean. Jon adjusts.
3. **Pilot the top 4–6 in parallel** as cheap versions (the easier problem):
   - rules or dilemma: a paper sim with `game-design-stages`, e.g. 50 simulated turns with a judge;
   - feel: a one-evening vertical slice (one screen per device, one TV moment) or a 60-second
     storyboard.

   A pilot answers one question: is the core loop fun and clear for a 5-year-old?
4. **Pick 2–4 to build** (Jon decides) and write their CHARTERs. For risky ones, split the first
   slice into 2–3 versions (see "Within one game" in SKILL.md).
5. **Keep the losers.** Their pilots and scores stay in IDEAS.md. A pruned idea can come back when
   a finding changes its risk (for example, a new art pipeline makes a look cheap).
